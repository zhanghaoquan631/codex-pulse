# Paper racing competition

The single-player game remains at `/racing/`. `/racing/competition/` offers Invite Friends and Challenge AI. Both create a six-car, three-lap room on APEX Circuit or Laguna Seca, with the player driving their own car. Challenge AI starts a race against five bots; friends replace available bot seats. Invitations carry only a random room ID; guest drivers receive a separate locally stored room token. Copying an invitation or displaying its QR code never sends a message automatically.

The original `/racing/` homepage also displays both choices directly beside its mode preview. These links carry the currently selected track and reuse the driver's name. The AI choice starts a human-driven race immediately; the friend choice creates the room and displays its invitation. The entry card stays out of active driving and competition sessions. Existing room links always open their room instead of creating another one.

This is simultaneous, non-contact racing. Original vehicle physics, barriers, models, audio and paper rendering remain in use. AI follows measured road geometry from the recovered game's complete replays and GLB road surfaces, including elevation and banking. All bots use the fixed nightmare speed/braking envelope, without catch-up slowdowns.

`server/racing/` owns clocks, progress, finish order, AI and participant authorization. D1 revision compare-and-swap preserves concurrent joins and updates. Only bot seats use AI driving. Inactive or disconnected human seats remain at their last validated progress, with stale speed zeroed; reconnecting and restarting keep the human in control. Old automatic-driving preferences are cleared when joining the updated game. Recovery resets speed at the last validated race distance. The podium can be settled while slower drivers finish their three laps.

The preserved minified engine imports `assets/competition-bridge.js`. Its hooks expose original physics and instanced opponents; the room client is `competition/session.js`. The bridge validates perspective projection after scene startup because the original menu transition may leave its aspect ratio invalid. Only invalid camera properties are repaired.

The engine also installs `assets/racing-audio.js` on the existing Howler implementation. Per-frame volume, rate and stereo writes are coalesced while audio is loading or awaiting a playback unlock. The latest values are applied through Howler when ready, preserving sounds without accumulating a recursive queue of stale parameter updates.

`npm test` exercises the real SQLite/D1 protocol, concurrent clients, authorization, human control retention, three-lap progress, results, road banking and the full Worker router. Motion tests cover variable latency, out-of-order snapshots, frame rate changes, packet loss, explicit recovery and finish stopping. `npm run db:generate` maintains the D1 migration chain. `npm run build` bundles the Worker and a local QR encoder; no external QR service is used.

Room snapshots expose the AI integration cursor separately from the response timestamp. The renderer continuously predicts bots along the actual road using the server speed envelope, with bounded corrections as snapshots arrive. This removes the old 200ms interpolation stop between network updates. Human opponents use bounded extrapolation; standings and finishes remain authoritative on the server. Network latency still affects responsiveness; this is not a lockstep physics or vehicle-collision server. No cash prizes or payments are involved. Room records expire after 24 hours.
